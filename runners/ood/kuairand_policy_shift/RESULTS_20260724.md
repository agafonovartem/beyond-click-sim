# KuaiRand natural policy-shift run, 2026-07-24

## Status

The 400-user, three-candidate-seed GPT-5.4-mini experiment completed with
released captions and category paths. A categories-only ablation, classical
baselines, no-history controls, and a 100-user permutation control also
completed. Every paid request parsed successfully.

This remains an exploratory result. The natural task parameters were chosen
after feasibility and aggregate label-distribution inspection, but before
model outcomes. Candidate seeds are correlated resamples for the same users,
not independent datasets.

## Locked task

- Dataset: KuaiRand-Pure, tab 1.
- Source history: 2022-04-22 through 2022-04-30.
- Validation targets: 2022-05-01 through 2022-05-03.
- Test targets: 2022-05-04 through 2022-05-08.
- Cohort: an outcome-blind 400-user subset shared by all methods and seeds.
- Visible history: exactly five natural exposures under one source policy.
- Target: exactly five later natural exposures under one target policy.
- Target selection: stable seeded hash independent of click labels.
- Target labels: released `is_click`; missing interactions are not negatives.
- Pair novelty: target user-item pairs did not occur in any earlier released
  tab or policy.
- Support: every target item has at least eight source exposure users under
  both policies and at least five users in each materialized training graph.
- LLM-visible fields: full released native-language description, category
  hierarchy, and the five history click labels. Raw IDs, policy names,
  timestamps, popularity, global CTR, and targets are hidden.

The target candidates are separately logged exposures rather than one
simultaneous slate. This is response prediction under an exposure-policy
shift, not off-policy policy-value estimation.

## What the controls say

### A real policy shift exists

The exposure-aware logistic baseline has 100% warm user/item coverage and uses
both clicked and non-clicked source exposures. Its transferred-minus-matched
log-loss penalty is:

- random history to standard target: +.2055, 95% CI [.1673, .2440];
- standard history to random target: +.2023, 95% CI [.1716, .2327].

The fixed Beta(1,1) five-label scalar baseline independently shows penalties
of .1114 and .1852. Thus the shift is not an artifact of a positive-only
recommender or zero-score fallback.

### GPT is less sensitive to source-policy mismatch, but this is not successful
behavioral extrapolation

For caption GPT, matched history beats transferred history on standard-target
log-loss by .0733, 95% CI [.0432, .1033]. The random-target difference is only
.0113, 95% CI [-.0092, .0320].

The apparently small GPT transfer gap is not sufficient evidence of
extrapolation. Transferred history is worse than no history in both target
regimes:

- random history on standard targets: quality delta -.0715,
  95% CI [-.0942, -.0490];
- standard history on random targets: quality delta -.0231,
  95% CI [-.0400, -.0064].

Here a positive quality delta means lower log-loss for the first condition.
The negative values mean that source-policy history hurts target-policy
probability prediction.

GPT beats exposure logistic on log-loss only in the standard-history to
random-target cell: +.1140, 95% CI [.0852, .1424]. It loses in the other three
cells. The isolated win is therefore best interpreted as a conservative
candidate/content prior avoiding the classical model's source-CTR
miscalibration, not as user-history transport.

### GPT changes its calibration in the expected source direction

Across all target rows, caption GPT predicts mean click probabilities of:

- .304 with standard history on standard targets;
- .254 with standard history on random targets;
- .219 with random history on standard targets;
- .205 with random history on random targets;
- .253 and .236 without history on standard and random targets.

Observed target CTRs are .452 and .206. The model therefore uses the visible
history to move its global response level toward the source regime. This helps
match the random-policy base rate but moves predictions in the wrong direction
under cross-policy transfer.

### Item-specific grounding is limited

The 100-user permutation control preserves every visible item, label multiset,
history length, and per-user click count, but reassigns labels to items.

- Matched original versus permuted history is unresolved in both target
  regimes.
- In random-history to standard-target transfer, original beats permuted by
  .0503 log-loss, 95% CI [.0164, .0868], and .0404 NDCG,
  95% CI [.0127, .0694].
- However, that original transferred history still loses to no history.

The model can ground some source feedback in item semantics, but the grounded
signal is not policy-invariant enough to improve target response prediction.

### Full captions do not rescue probability extrapolation

Against categories only, captions:

- improve no-history standard-target log-loss by .0878,
  95% CI [.0670, .1091];
- produce small NDCG gains in several history cells, including +.0214 for
  random-history to standard-target, 95% CI [.0078, .0352];
- do not improve either transferred-history log-loss after Holm correction;
- worsen matched-cell log-loss numerically, by .0400 on standard targets and
  .0335 on random targets.

Thus the released descriptions add candidate-level ranking information, but
they do not solve cross-policy calibration or behavioral transport.

## Interpretation

This is a useful negative OOD result:

1. The same users and warm items are evaluated under a real standard-versus-
   randomized exposure shift.
2. Classical probability models show a large transfer penalty.
3. GPT appears more robust in the narrow sense of having a smaller
   matched-to-transferred gap.
4. No-history and permutation controls reveal why that is not sufficient:
   transferred history worsens calibrated prediction, while much of GPT's
   useful signal comes from candidate semantics and a conservative prior.

The defensible paper claim is:

> A small OOD performance gap does not by itself demonstrate behavioral
> extrapolation. Under KuaiRand exposure-policy shift, GPT-5.4 mini changes its
> predictions with source history and sometimes uses item-feedback
> associations, yet transferred history degrades natural-rate probability
> prediction relative to no history.

It is not defensible to claim that GPT generally beats classical recommenders
or successfully adapts to the recommendation-policy change.
